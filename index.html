Apply principles of sustainability to create viable systems

During my internship involving micro anechoic chamber examination, I gained critical insights into the intersection of advanced engineering practices and sustainability principles. The micro anechoic chamber, designed to absorb electromagnetic waves and eliminate external noise interference, plays a vital role in testing electronic devices with precision. While the technical focus of my work centered on signal integrity and noise reduction, I began to reflect on how such systems could be optimized for long-term viability through sustainable design and operation.

One of the key sustainability principles I applied was resource efficiency. The chamber’s construction materials involving specialized foams and absorptive surfaces, which requires careful selection to minimize environmental impact while maintaining performance. I participated in discussions about alternative materials that could reduce carbon footprint without compromising functionality. Additionally, energy consumption during testing procedures emerged as a critical concern. By analyzing power usage patterns and proposing scheduling adjustments to reduce idle time, I contributed to a more energy-conscious workflow.

Systems thinking also guided my reflection. The micro anechoic chamber is part of a broader ecosystem of testing, manufacturing, and product development. Understanding its role within this network allowed me to identify opportunities for circularity, such as repurposing outdated components or integrating modular designs that extend the chamber’s lifecycle. This experience reinforced the importance of embedding sustainability into engineering systems from the outset. Viable systems are not only technically sound but also environmentally responsible and adaptable to future needs. By aligning the chamber’s operational goals with sustainability principles—efficiency, longevity, and minimal ecological impact, I recognized the potential for innovation that supports both technological advancement and planetary stewardship. This internship has deepened my commitment to designing systems that are not only effective but also ethically and ecologically sustainable.
